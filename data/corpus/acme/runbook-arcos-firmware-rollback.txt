RUNBOOK - ArcOS FIRMWARE ROLLBACK (SKYLIFT OTA)
================================================

Owner ......... Controls Software team (lead: Hector Villanueva)
Applies to .... ARC-6 / ARC-12 / ARC-20 and Kite controllers on ArcOS 4.x
Last updated .. 2026-09-02
Audience ...... engineering, platform admins ONLY. Do not share with customers.

WHEN TO USE THIS
----------------
Use this runbook when a firmware release pushed through Skylift (our OTA service) is causing problems in the field: unexpected faults, controllers failing to boot, safety controller mismatch errors, or a spike in support tickets that started right after a rollout.

Skylift rollouts are staged: 1% of connected controllers, then 10%, then 50%, then 100%, with at least 24 hours between stages. In most cases you are rolling back a partial stage, which is much easier than rolling back everything.

IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTE
A firmware rollback reboots the controller. Never trigger a rollback on a robot that might be mid-cycle without the customer's knowledge. Skylift only applies a rollback when the controller reports state IDLE or E-STOPPED, but customers must still be notified before you schedule it.

STEP 1 - PAUSE THE ROLLOUT
--------------------------
Stop the bleeding first.

    skylift rollout pause --release arcos-4.8.2

This stops any additional controllers from receiving the release. It does not change controllers that already updated.

Post in #controls-release:  "Paused arcos-4.8.2 at <stage>%, investigating <symptom>."

STEP 2 - CONFIRM THE RELEASE IS THE CAUSE
-----------------------------------------
  a) In FleetSight admin, filter fault events by firmware version. Compare the fault rate of controllers on the new version vs the previous version over the same window.
  b) Look for the specific fault codes. Common ones after a bad release:
       F-2107  joint encoder checksum
       F-3310  safety controller CRC mismatch
       F-0412  watchdog reset loop
  c) If the fault rate on the new version is more than 3x the previous version, treat the release as bad and continue to Step 3.

STEP 3 - DECLARE AN INCIDENT
----------------------------
A bad firmware release that is still pushing to customers is a SEV1 per the Incident Response Process. A paused rollout with limited field impact is normally SEV2. The Incident Commander makes the final call.

STEP 4 - ROLL BACK
------------------
The previous signed image is always retained on the controller's B partition, so a rollback does not need to download anything.

    skylift rollback create --release arcos-4.8.2 --target-version arcos-4.8.1 --cohort affected
    skylift rollback status --id <rollback-id>

Rollbacks require approval from TWO people: the on-call controls engineer and either the Controls Software lead or a platform admin. Skylift enforces this; the second approver runs:

    skylift rollback approve --id <rollback-id>

Expected time: most controllers revert within 20 minutes of reaching IDLE. Controllers that are offline will roll back the next time they connect.

STEP 5 - AIR-GAPPED / USB SITES
-------------------------------
Sites without FleetSight do not get OTA updates and therefore are only affected if a technician installed the release by USB. Customer Support will contact those customers and ship (or email a link to) the previous signed USB image. Do not send unsigned builds to anyone, ever.

STEP 6 - VERIFY
---------------
  - Fault rate for the affected cohort back to baseline for 2 hours
  - No controllers stuck in "rollback pending" for more than 24 hours (follow up with support for those)
  - Release marked BLOCKED in Skylift so nobody re-starts it by accident:
        skylift release block --release arcos-4.8.2 --reason "<jira ticket>"

STEP 7 - FOLLOW UP
------------------
Open a Jira ticket in CTRL with the label POSTMORTEM and link the incident channel. The fix ships as a new version number (e.g. 4.8.3). We never re-publish a version number that was rolled back.
