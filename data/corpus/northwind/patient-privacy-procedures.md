# Patient Privacy Procedures (HIPAA) - Workforce Guide

Owner: Privacy Office (Nadia Farouk, Chief Privacy Officer)
Last Updated: 9/30/2025

This guide turns our HIPAA privacy policies into day-to-day procedures. It applies to everyone at Northwind Health who can see, hear, or handle patient information - which is nearly everyone.

## The basics in one paragraph

Protected health information (PHI) is any information that can identify a patient and relates to their health, care, or payment for care. Names, dates of birth, phone numbers, MRNs, appointment times and even the fact that someone is a patient are all PHI. You may use or share PHI only for treatment, payment, or health care operations, or when the patient authorizes it or the law requires it. Use the **minimum necessary** information to do your job.

## Verifying identity

Before sharing any information with a patient by phone or in person, verify **two identifiers**: full name plus date of birth, or full name plus address. For telehealth visits, the clinician verifies identity at the start of every visit and confirms the patient's physical location (state).

## Talking to family members and others

- If the patient is present and agrees (or doesn't object), you may discuss their care with family or friends who are with them.
- If the patient is not present, check the chart for a signed **Authorization for Release** or a proxy designation. No authorization, no information - even for spouses.
- Parents generally have access to records of minors, with important exceptions for adolescents 12-17 seeking confidential services (reproductive health, mental health, substance use). Adolescent confidential notes are flagged in the EHR and hidden from the proxy portal.

## Common situations

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| A caller says they're from an insurance company and asks for records | Transfer to Health Information Management (HIM); do not release yourself |
| Law enforcement asks about a patient | Do not confirm or deny the patient is here. Call the clinic manager and the Privacy Office |
| A coworker asks you to look up their lab result | No. They can view it in the app or ask their own clinician |
| You see a neighbor in the waiting room | Do not mention it to anyone, ever |
| A fax went to the wrong number | Report it to the Privacy Office within 1 hour |

## Front desk and waiting rooms

1. Call patients by first name only in waiting rooms.
2. Keep sign-in tablets angled away from the waiting area.
3. Do not leave schedules, labels or wristbands visible at the desk.
4. Speak quietly when confirming information at the counter.

## Sending PHI

- Internal email inside Northwind is encrypted and acceptable for PHI when necessary.
- Email to anyone outside Northwind must be sent with **[secure]** in the subject line.
- Faxing: use a cover sheet, confirm the number, and use pre-programmed numbers for frequent recipients.
- Never send PHI through personal email or text messages.

## Patient rights requests

Patients have the right to access their records, request amendments, request restrictions, get an accounting of disclosures, and choose how we contact them. All of these requests go to HIM. Records access requests must be fulfilled within 15 business days (which is faster than the law requires; we chose that standard).

## Reporting a privacy concern

If you think PHI was used or shared improperly - by you or anyone else - report it to the Privacy Office within 1 hour of discovery: privacy@northwindhealth.example or the Integrity Line, 1-800-555-0199. The Privacy Office decides whether it is a reportable breach. Do not try to investigate on your own and do not contact the patient about it yourself.

## Sanctions

Privacy violations are handled on a tiered scale:

- Level 1 (accidental, low risk): coaching and retraining
- Level 2 (careless or repeated): written warning
- Level 3 (intentional access without a work reason): final warning or termination
- Level 4 (access for personal gain or malice): termination and possible referral to authorities

Remember: every chart access is logged. "I was just curious" is a Level 3 violation.
